### 1. California Right of Publicity (Most Important)

- **California Civil Code § 3344**
  - Common-law “right of publicity”
  - This basically says you generally cannot use someone’s likeness:
    - to advertise,
    - sell products,
    - imply endorsement,
    - or commercially exploit them
      without permission.

- For a meme app, this matters if:
  - you monetize celebrity swaps,
  - run ads using celebrity faces,
  - let users generate fake endorsements,
  - or market the app with recognizable people.

- Is Fine to Use For
  - parody,
  - satire,
  - obvious jokes,
  - transformative memes,
  - noncommercial experimentation.

## 2. Deepfake Laws (California + Federal)

- California already has targeted deepfake laws.
  - **California AB 602**
    - (nonconsensual sexual deepfakes)
  - **California AB 730**
    - (election-related deepfakes)
  - Federal **TAKE IT DOWN Act** (2025)

- Absolutely avoid:
  - NSFW face swaps without explicit consent
  - political deception
  - fake revenge content
  - minors
  - harassment/stalking uses

- For A Safer APP, Build
  - moderation,
  - reporting,
  - takedown tools,
  - and filters against explicit content.

## 3. Copyright Law

- Faces themselves are not copyrighted.
- But the below are:
  - photos,
  - movie clips,
  - TV scenes,
  - and source media

- So if users upload:
  - Disney screenshots,
  - Marvel scenes,
  - TikTok clips,
  - Netflix footage,
- copyright law may apply.

- Read the Below to know more:
  - **Fair Use doctrine**
  - DMCA safe harbor
  - Copyright Act §512

- If you host user uploads publicly, DMCA compliance matters a lot.
  - You should likely:
    - register a DMCA agent,
    - have takedown procedures,
    - publish a repeat infringer policy.

## 4. Defamation & False Endorsement

- You can get into trouble if your swaps imply:
  - someone committed crimes,
  - endorsed products,
  - said fake things,
  - or participated in explicit acts.

- Read the below to know more:
  - defamation basics,
  - Lanham Act false endorsement law.

## 5. FTC / Consumer Protection

- If AI content could fool users, disclosure becomes important.
  - Especially if:
    - ads are involved,
    - influencers are involved,
    - or the content looks highly realistic.

- A smart practice:
  - watermark AI content,
  - label swaps as synthetic,
  - avoid deceptive realism.

---

## 6. Terms of Service & Consent Design

- Operationally, these matter as much as laws.
- You should have:
  - Terms of Service
  - Acceptable Use Policy
  - DMCA Policy
  - Privacy Policy
  - Takedown process

- And ideally:
- user attestation:
  “I have the rights/consent to upload this image.”

## Practical “Safe-ish Meme App” Rules

### For Lower Risk:

- obvious parody
- stylized/cartoon outputs
- opt-in swaps among friends
- consent-only uploads
- watermark outputs
- no NSFW
- no political deepfakes
- no fake ads
- no celebrity endorsement simulation
